We have all been on the receiving end of feedback that leaves us more confused than when we started. Comments like "try to be more proactive" or "this presentation needs a bit more polish" sound well-intentioned, yet they offer zero direction on what to actually change. When feedback lacks specificity, it forces the recipient to guess at expectations, which often leads to frustration rather than growth.

Giving truly useful feedback is one of the most valuable skills you can develop in the workplace, yet many of us avoid it out of fear of sounding overly critical or damaging a good working relationship. The secret is shifting your focus from judging the person to observing specific behaviors and their impact. In this post, we will break down a simple, repeatable framework you can use to deliver constructive feedback that your colleagues will genuinely appreciate.
